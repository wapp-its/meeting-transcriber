# Live levels for every device in the Audio Devices window

## Conversation Evidence

The fork is public, so the owner's turns are paraphrased, not quoted.

> owner (paraphrased, issue #256, 2026-11-16): the Device Details window from #150 (menu bar, Audio Devices, "Device Details…") shows level bars only during a recording; it should be the place to find which device is in use.
> owner (paraphrased, 2026-11-16): while the window is open, every microphone and every output device shows a live level bar, so before a call one can speak and see which microphone moves, or play a sound and see which output moves.
> owner (paraphrased, 2026-11-16): while a recording runs or a watched meeting app is in a call, the window opens nothing extra: only the two recorded tracks keep their bars, and every other row says it is paused for the call.
> owner (paraphrased, 2026-11-16): live metering must not hurt system performance badly.
> owner (paraphrased, 2026-11-16): accepts that a Bluetooth headset switches to its call profile while the window is open outside a call.
> owner (paraphrased, 2026-11-16): rejected a per-device Test button (fork #248) in favour of this.

## Goal & Context

<!-- Source: 45% [paraphrase] / 55% [inferred] -->

Before a call it is hard to tell which microphone hears you and which output plays the call. The Audio Devices window (gh-150) lists every microphone and output with its marks, but shows a level only for the two tracks a running recording captures. After this spec, while the window is visible and no call or recording runs, every microphone row shows what that microphone hears and every output row shows what the other apps play to that output, as a live 12-segment bar. Speak, and the microphone in use moves; play a sound, and the output in use moves.

A call is the one time the window must not touch audio devices: opening an input switches a Bluetooth headset's profile, and a profile switch underneath a recording's freshly built app-audio tap leaves that tap silent (`docs/architecture/recording.md`, start order, issue #693). So while a recording runs or a watched meeting app signals a call, the window closes every device it opened before any capture starts, keeps only the recording's two bars from gh-150, and says on every other row that its level is paused.

This replaces gh-150's rule that the window never opens a device (its R5 and D3) for the time outside a call; during a call or recording that rule still holds.

## Architecture & Data Models

- **One owner of the window's meters**, separate from the recorder and from `ChannelHealthController`: it opens one meter per listed device while the window is visible and no pause signal is on, keeps one level per meter, and closes all of them on close, hide or pause. The window's existing reader (`AudioDevicesWindowReader`) samples those levels on its 100 ms tick and stores lit segments only on a change, as it does for the recorded levels; the state value (`AudioDevicesWindowState`) decides every row's level column, so every arm stays testable without the window. The meters feed nothing else: no channel-health monitor, no file, no log of levels. [inferred]
- **Inputs:** one Core Audio IOProc per microphone, on the device itself (`AudioDeviceCreateIOProcIDWithBlock`, `AudioDeviceStart`), which computes each buffer's level over all channels in the callback and stores one number, with no allocation, lock or log there. No `AVAudioEngine` per device: an engine adds a graph, a converter and the `inputNode` bring-up that can wedge (issue #588) or bind a stale format (issue #724), none of which a level needs. Cost per device: one real-time IO thread at the device's own buffer size, no conversion, no file. Opening an input turns on macOS's orange microphone indicator for as long as a meter runs, and moves a Bluetooth headset into its call profile (D4). [inferred]
- **Outputs:** one process tap per output device, created from `CATapDescription`'s device-stream initialiser that excludes processes (this app's own process object excluded, the device's UID, stream 0); the SDK documents it as mixing "all process audio streams destined for the selected device stream except the given processes". A tap is read only through an aggregate device (the SDK's `kAudioTapPropertyFormat` is "the format of the data that will be accessible in any aggregate device that contains the tap"), so each output gets a private aggregate described the way the recording describes its own (`AppAudioCapture.aggregateDescription`: private, the tapped device as main and only sub-device, the tap with drift compensation, tap auto-start) and one IOProc that reads the tap's buffer, the last one (`AppAudioCapture.tapBuffer(in:)`, issue #803: an output that also has inputs exposes them first, so metering a headset's output also runs its microphone). Under tap auto-start an aggregate whose device plays nothing stays dormant and delivers no buffers (measured for the recording's tap, `NoFirstBufferProbeSchedule`); for a meter that is an empty bar, which is correct. Rejected: one aggregate holding every output's tap (one IO thread, but one device leaving disturbs every output meter; the fallback if the budget is missed), a global tap (no level per device), and the running flag alone (says running, not how loud). [inferred]
- **The output tap's grant** is the recording tap's: the "Audio Recording" grant (`NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription`) or, as a fallback, Screen Recording. With neither, the tap is created without error and delivers silence (issue #524). The Audio Recording grant has no preflight and `PermissionHealthCheck` models only Screen Recording, so a missing grant and a quiet device look the same (A12). [inferred]
- **The pause signal** is on while either holds: (a) recording activity, `WatchingController.isRecordingActivity`: a manual recording from its registration (before its recorder is up), a detected meeting from its capture start, both until the recorder has stopped, paused included, pushed in the same main-actor turn it changes; (b) a watched app's call signal, `MeetingDetecting.signallingMeetings()`: every watched identity whose call signal is present now, without waiting for confirmation, cooldowns included and deny-listed apps skipped, which covers a call being confirmed, an open consent question, a held app and a call the person chose not to record (A4, A5). [inferred]
- **The capture gate:** a detected meeting records at least one poll after its first call signal (two confirmations, 3 s apart by default), so its meters are closed long before. A manual recording, a microphone-only one through `/v1/record` and Record Again can start with no call signal before them, and the detected path sets the `.recording` phase before it awaits `recorderFactory()` while the manual path sets it after `recorder.start`. So every start path awaits one gate right before `recorder.start` that returns once the meters are closed, at once when none is open (A6). [inferred]
- **"In use" on a metered device:** `kAudioDevicePropertyDeviceIsRunningSomewhere` is true when the device runs "in at least one process on the system" (SDK header), so the meter itself would light gh-150's In use mark on every row it opens. On a device a meter holds, In use comes from the process objects instead: some process other than this app lists the device in its `kAudioProcessPropertyDevices` (input or output scope) while it runs audio, with an aggregate it uses counting for that aggregate's active sub-devices. A device no meter holds keeps gh-150's running flag, so during a call or recording the mark means what it meant before (A7). [inferred]
- **The app's own audio is never a call.** While meters run, this app's process is one of the processes `MicInputDetector` enumerates as running input. No built-in pattern matches this app's bundle and the miss log already skips it, but `MicInputDetector.claim(_:)` does not exclude it, so an added watched app whose bundle ID matches this app's would read the meters as a call, and the pause would then switch the meters off and on again. Detection therefore never claims this app's own process (R5). `PowerAssertionDetector` reads `IOPMCopyAssertionsByProcess`; coreaudiod holds audio IO assertions under its own process name, and their names match no pattern (the browser pattern needs "WebRTC" or "PeerConnection" in the name), so the meters cannot reach it. [inferred]
- **The device lists ignore the meters' own objects.** This app's process sees its own private aggregates in `kAudioHardwarePropertyDevices`, which `OutputDevices.observeChanges` listens to, so each meter aggregate created or destroyed fires the Output entry's refresh. `OutputDevices.available()` already drops private aggregates (they report `kAudioDevicePropertyDeviceCanBeDefaultDevice` false, measured). The meters rebuild only when a filtered list changes, never on the notice alone, so creating a meter never triggers another rebuild (R7). [inferred]
- **Off the main thread, one at a time:** every meter open and close runs on one serial lane, each bounded by a two-second deadline like gh-150's readings and the capture restarts (`RestartArbiter`): an overdue call leaves the window responsive, is torn down when it returns, and no further call queues behind it (R8). [inferred]
- **Expected, not measured:** while a meter runs, coreaudiod holds the idle-sleep assertion it holds for any running audio IO, so a metering window keeps the Mac from idle sleep; this is one reason the meters run only while the window is visible (A15). [inferred]

## API Contracts

- The debug `/state` snapshot's `audioDevicesWindow` keeps every field gh-150 gave it and gains `visible`, `meters` and a `level` per row: `{ "open": true, "visible": true, "meters": { "state": "running", "pauseReason": null, "openInputs": 3, "openOutputs": 2, "overdue": 0 }, "rows": [ { "group": "microphone", "name": "USB Headset", "marks": ["inUse"], "meetingApp": null, "levelBar": true, "litSegments": 7, "level": "live" }, { "group": "output", "name": "MacBook Pro Speakers", "marks": ["default"], "meetingApp": null, "levelBar": false, "level": "cannotRead" } ], "selfRunningInput": true }`. `meters.state` is `off` (window closed or not visible), `running` or `paused`; `pauseReason` is `recording`, `call` or null, `recording` winning when both hold; `openInputs` and `openOutputs` count the meters open right now; `overdue` counts lane calls past their deadline that have not returned. `level` is `live` (a meter's bar), `recorded` (gh-150's bar from the recording), `paused`, `cannotRead`, `noPermission`, `unknown` or `none` (a row that has no level, such as a configured microphone that is not connected). `levelBar` and `litSegments` keep gh-150's meaning: a bar shows for `live` and `recorded`. `selfRunningInput` keeps gh-150's meaning (this app's process runs audio input); with the meters it is true while input meters run and false while paused outside a recording. Built from the window's state and the meters' and reader's last results with no Core Audio call. Not part of `/v1`. [inferred]
- No new setting, no change to `/v1`, no change to the recorder's own capture. [inferred]

## Edge Cases & Constraints

- **Window not visible** (closed, minimised, fully covered or on another Space): no meter open (A15). [inferred]
- **Hot-plug while open:** a new device gets a meter within one second unless paused; a removed one's meter closes and its row goes; a meter whose device disappears during its open is closed when the open returns. A change of the default input or output reopens no other meter. [inferred]
- **A device that cannot be opened** (the open or start fails, the device is in exclusive use, the sandbox refuses it): its row shows "Can't read" and no bar; it is tried again at the next device-list change or the next time the window becomes visible, never on a timer. [inferred]
- **coreaudiod not answering** (gh-150's R6 rule, issue #588): rows whose meter call is overdue show "Unknown"; the window, `/state` and a recording start never wait longer than the deadline. [inferred]
- **Microphone permission denied:** microphone rows show "No permission" and no input opens; not yet asked: the first open asks, as macOS does. [inferred]
- **Output grant missing** (the tap delivers silence, which cannot be told from a quiet device): output bars run as usual, and while `PermissionHealthCheck` reports Screen Recording not healthy a grey hint line under Outputs names the grant (A12). [inferred]
- **Back-to-back calls:** the meters stay closed for ten seconds after the pause signal last cleared, so the gap between two calls of the same app (3 to 9 s for Teams, `docs/architecture/detection.md`) does not reopen them (A5). [inferred]
- **The window opened during a call:** it opens nothing; the rows show their paused text from the first frame. [inferred]
- **App quit while metering:** the meters close with the app; the process's private taps and aggregates go with it. [inferred]
- **Privacy:** no device name or UID is logged; the log carries counts, reasons and status codes only (A14). Levels are numbers, never audio: nothing is written to disk. [inferred]
- **Both build variants** get the meters; whatever the App Store sandbox refuses shows as "Can't read". [inferred]

## Acceptance Criteria

- **R1:** While the window is visible and the pause signal is off, every microphone row and every output row shows a live 12-segment bar (gh-150's A9 mapping) from its own meter, updated at least ten times a second; a microphone bar follows what that microphone hears and an output bar what apps other than this one play to that output. Errors: a device that cannot be opened → "Can't read", no bar, tried again only at the next device-list change or the next time the window becomes visible; microphone permission denied → "No permission" and no input opened; an overdue open → "Unknown"; a configured microphone that is not connected → no bar; a device that goes quiet → no lit segment within one second. [paraphrase]
- **R2:** While the pause signal is on (a recording from its registration until its recorder has stopped, paused included, or a watched app's call signal as `signallingMeetings()` reports it, watching on or off), no meter is open: every meter open when the signal turns on is closed within one second, and none opens until the signal has been off for ten seconds. The recorded microphone's row and the Meeting audio row keep gh-150's recorded bars; every other row shows "Paused for call", or "Paused for recording" during a recording with no app tapped and no call signal. Errors: a close past its deadline → its rows show "Unknown", the window stays responsive and R3 still bounds the recording start; a deny-listed app's call is not a call signal (A4). [paraphrase]
- **R3:** Every recording start path (a detected meeting, an approved consent answer, Record Again, Record App, a microphone-only recording, `/v1/record`) waits, right before its recorder opens any device, until every meter is closed, and never longer than the bound in A6; with no meter open it does not wait at all. Errors: the bound passes with a meter still open → the recording starts anyway and one log line says so. [inferred]
- **R4:** Closing the window or the window ceasing to be visible (A15) closes every meter within one second, and nothing stays open afterwards; becoming visible again reopens them within one second unless paused. Errors: a close past its deadline → torn down when it returns, and `meters.overdue` counts it until then; a reopen while an old close is still out waits for it. [paraphrase]
- **R5:** No detector reports a meeting or a call signal because of this app's own audio: `MicInputDetector` never claims this app's process, also when an added watched app's bundle ID equals or prefixes this app's, so the meters can never pause themselves. No error surface beyond that. [inferred]
- **R6:** On a device a meter holds, "In use" shows only while a process other than this app runs audio on it (an aggregate such a process uses counts for its active sub-devices); this app's meters never light it. A device no meter holds keeps gh-150's R3 rule. Errors: a failed or late process read → "Status unknown", as in gh-150. [inferred]
- **R7:** Creating or destroying a meter's taps and aggregates changes neither device list, adds no row, and starts no meter rebuild; the meters rebuild only when the filtered microphone or output list changes. No error surface beyond R9. [inferred]
- **R8:** Every meter open and close runs off the main thread on one lane, one call at a time, each with a two-second deadline; no call queues behind an overdue one; `/state` and the window never wait on the lane. No error surface beyond R1's "Unknown" and R4's overdue count. [inferred]
- **R9:** Hot-plug while the window is open: a device added gets its row and, unless paused, its meter within one second; a device removed loses its row and its meter is closed; a default change reopens no other meter. Errors: a device removed while its open is out → closed when the open returns. [inferred]
- **R10:** The performance budget in A9 holds, measured live in the dev app as Validation step 6 says, and the numbers measured are recorded in `docs/architecture-macos.md`. Errors: a budget missed → the build stops and reports the numbers rather than shipping. [paraphrase]
- **R11:** `/state.audioDevicesWindow` carries `visible`, `meters` and each row's `level` as in API Contracts, built with no Core Audio call. Errors: window closed → `open: false`, `visible: false`, `meters.state: "off"`, `rows: []`. [inferred]
- **R12:** The window's level column, footer and hint lines read as the accepted mockup boards and A13; the footer outside a call says that levels pause during calls and that macOS shows the microphone as in use while levels show. No error surface beyond R1 and R2's texts. [inferred]
- **R13:** `docs/architecture/recording.md` states the capture gate and the meters' place in the start order, `docs/architecture/detection.md` states that detection never claims this app's own process, and `docs/architecture-macos.md` describes the meters, their lane and their budget. No error surface. [inferred]

## Boundaries

- No per-device Test button (D5). [paraphrase]
- No switching devices in the window; gh-150's D6 stands. [inferred]
- No change to what or how a recording captures; the recording's two bars keep coming from the recording (gh-150 R4). [inferred]
- No meter while a call or recording runs (D2). [paraphrase]
- No new setting, and no change to the menu's Microphone and Output entries. [inferred]
- No audio from a meter is kept or written anywhere. [inferred]

## Parked unknowns

- Whether a device tap's private aggregate needs the tapped device as its sub-device, or runs on the tap alone (which would avoid running a headset's microphone for its output meter); the build's first step measures it on one output with inputs.
- Whether macOS shows its system-audio recording indicator while an output tap runs; seen in the live check of Validation step 6.
- How long a Bluetooth headset takes to return to its music profile after the meter closes its input, and whether a recording started inside that time loses its app track; Validation step 7 measures it, and A6's settle wait follows the measurement.
- Whether `AVCaptureDevice` discovery (the microphone list) lists a private aggregate that carries a tap; R7's test against the live list decides whether the microphone list needs its own filter.
