## Goal & Context

Today a recording can only be stopped. A confidential stretch of a meeting or a break therefore ends up in the recording, or the user stops and starts again and gets two transcripts and two protocols for one meeting. The user wants to pause a running recording and resume it later: nothing is captured while it is paused, and the meeting still ends up as one transcript and one protocol, with a line in the transcript showing where and when the pause was. [paraphrase]

The control has to work where the user is during a call: the menu bar menu, and the local automation API that a Stream Deck key calls. The menu bar icon has to show that the recording is paused, so nobody believes they are recording while they are not, or the reverse. [paraphrase]

The app records two tracks at once, the meeting app's audio and the microphone, and later lines them up against each other for speaker assignment and echo detection. A pause must remove the same span of time from both, or every step that compares the two tracks breaks after the first pause. [paraphrase]

## Architecture & Data Models

- **Where a pause takes effect: the capture library's write path.** During a pause the capture session keeps both devices open and keeps receiving buffers, but cuts out of every buffer exactly the frames whose capture time falls inside the pause, before they reach the resampler or converter, the track file or the live-caption sink. The cut is frame-exact rather than per buffer, because the two tracks deliver buffers of different sizes at different moments (the app tap about 10 ms, the microphone up to about 170 ms): dropping whole buffers would remove a different span from each track and shift them against each other by up to a buffer per pause. No file is closed and nothing is restarted, so a pause cannot trigger a device or Bluetooth profile change and cannot hit the start-order race that opening a capture has (issue #693, see `AudioCaptureSession.start()`). Pausing cannot fail: it marks the session's pause timeline, which every buffer consults, including buffers of a capture that restarts during the pause. [inferred]
- **One pause clock for both tracks.** `AudioCaptureSession` owns one pause timeline in host time (mach ticks in seconds, the clock both `AudioTimeStamp.mHostTime` and `AVAudioTime.hostTime` use) and hands it to both `AppAudioCapture` and `MicCaptureHandler`. Each track asks it, per buffer, "which of this buffer's frames lie outside every pause?" (from the buffer's presentation time, frame count and rate) and "where is this instant on the pause-free recording clock?". The recording clock is host time minus all paused time before that instant; it stands still during a pause. Each track writes its kept frames piece by piece and feeds its `TimelineAnchor` (which today fills real device-restart gaps with silence) the recording-clock position of each piece instead of raw host time, so a pause becomes no gap at all: the audio before and after it is written back to back, and both tracks lose exactly the paused span. Without a pause the recording clock equals host time, so an unpaused recording is written exactly as today. [inferred]
- **Alignment and positions.** Both tracks remove the same host-time interval, so they stay aligned. The session reports `micDelay` (difference of the two tracks' first frames) on the recording clock, and it reports each pause's position on the saved tracks' timeline: the pause start on the recording clock minus the earliest first frame of the recorded tracks, which is the origin of the transcript timeline after the recorder's mic-delay normalisation and the dual-source merge. These positions are exact even when the Mac slept during the recording, because the host clock and the audio both stop during sleep. [inferred]
- **The pause state lives in `WatchLoop`.** `WatchLoop` gets an observable `pausedSince: Date?` (part of `WatchLoopState`) and `isPaused`. It is set only by an explicit pause, and it is non-nil only while the loop is in `.recording`; every transition out of `.recording` clears it. It is the single answer to "is the recording paused" for the menu, the icon, the automation API and later logic that measures silence or recorded time. The recorder mirrors it as `RecordingProvider.isPaused`, which the channel-health polling reads. Pause and resume run on the main actor, like start and stop. [inferred]
- **The pause record.** `RecordingPause` holds the wall-clock `startedAt` and `endedAt` (for the marker text) and `offsetSeconds` (the position on the saved timeline). `WatchLoop` keeps a pure `RecordingPauseLog` of wall-clock pause and resume times for the running recording; at the stop it pairs them, in order, with the positions the capture reported. Only when the capture reported none (a recorder double) or a different count does it fall back to wall-clock arithmetic (time since the recording started minus earlier paused time). A pause still open at the stop ends at the stop. The list travels on the `PipelineJob` (persisted, so a restart mid-pipeline keeps it) and into the record-only sidecar. [inferred]
- **One conversion for cuts.** `[RecordingPause].pausedDuration(from: Date, to: Date) -> TimeInterval` is the paused time inside an interval, `[RecordingPause].timelineOffset(of: Date, recordingStartedAt: Date) -> TimeInterval` maps a wall-clock time onto the pause-free saved timeline (time since the start minus paused time before it; it stands still inside a pause), and `[RecordingPause].clipped(at: Date)` drops pauses that start at or after a cut and ends a pause spanning the cut at the cut. gh-54's cut uses them in this spec (each of its wall-clock position estimates subtracts the paused time inside the interval it spans); gh-45's cut uses the same helpers. [inferred]
- **The transcript marker is rendered, not stored in the segments.** The transcript text is rendered in one place, `[TimestampedSegment].transcriptText(note:)`, from segments plus a recording-level note taken from the job. The pauses join the note as a second input: the rendering puts one line `[Pause HH:MM–HH:MM]` (local 24-hour time, en dash) before the first rendered line that starts at or after the pause's offset, and merging consecutive same-speaker segments never merges across a pause offset. Every rendering (the transcription-stage draft, the speaker-labelled rewrite, the late re-diarization rebuild) goes through it with the job's pauses, so the marker appears in every saved transcript and in the text the protocol is generated from. [inferred]
- **Channel health is held while paused.** While the recorder reports paused, the channel-health polling does not feed its monitors: no red channel tint, no "Recording Appears Silent", no "Capture Channel Silent" / "Capture Channel Lost" / rebuilds-exhausted notification. At resume the monitors start from zero (their debounce windows and the fault monitors' since-start clock restart), so they judge only what happens after the resume; a fault already reported before the pause is not reported again, and a channel the capture gave up on during the pause is reported on the first check after the resume. [inferred]
- **Controls.** The menu gets one Pause/Resume item while a recording's capture is running. The menu bar icon gets a paused mark as an overlay flag on the icon renderer, like the watching dot: while it is set the icon draws a static pause symbol in place of the waveform (no animation); the watching dot, the record-only dot and the permission badge draw over it as today. `BadgeKind` stays unchanged, so the `badge` value the `/v1` API reports keeps saying `recording` during a pause (the `/v1` badge values carry a stability contract; the planned specs gh-49 and gh-58 add their icon marks the same way). `POST /v1/record` gets the verbs `pause` and `resume`, the record status gets `paused`, and the debug `/state` snapshot gets `recordingPaused`. [paraphrase]
- **Issue #45 (ask to stop after long silence, spec gh-45, planned in parallel and also built on gh-54).** Paused time must never count as silence there, and its "last speech plus 10 s" cut must be mapped onto the pause-free timeline. This spec provides the readable pause state (`WatchLoop.isPaused` / `pausedSince`, `RecordingProvider.isPaused`) and the conversion above. Whichever of the two specs lands second wires them together: if gh-45 is already merged when this spec is built, this spec holds gh-45's silence count while paused (the count restarts at resume) and routes gh-45's cut through the conversion; otherwise gh-45 does both. [inferred]
- **Dependency on gh-54.** gh-54 (ask before auto-stopping, being built now) cuts a detected meeting's saved audio back to "signal lost plus end grace" when its countdown ends unanswered or with "Stop now". Its cut converts the cut time into a position on the saved timeline from wall-clock times (counted from the recording's start, and back from its stop against the mix length); with pauses each must subtract the paused time inside the interval it spans, or the cut lands in the wrong place: too late keeps room audio after the meeting, which gh-54 exists to prevent, too early cuts meeting audio. This spec adapts that conversion and its loop changes build after gh-54 is merged. [inferred]

## Edge Cases & Constraints

- **Frame accuracy.** Which frames are paused is decided from each buffer's presentation time (the host time of its first frame) plus the frame index over the buffer's own sample rate, so the pause boundaries are as exact as the capture's timestamps; a buffer that straddles a boundary is split, and a pause shorter than one buffer removes only its own frames from that buffer. [inferred]
- **Pause before capture is up.** An auto-detected recording enters `.recording` a moment before its recorder has started. In that moment the menu offers no Pause item, and the API answers `pause` with 409; nothing pretends to pause. [inferred]
- **Pause while a track has not delivered its first buffer yet** (a slow app tap): the track's timeline, `micDelay` and the pause positions still come out right, because all are taken on the recording clock, which stands still during the pause. [inferred]
- **Device change or capture restart during a pause:** handled by the existing restart paths; the restarted capture is the same handler instance and keeps the same pause timeline. A restart gap that overlaps a pause is filled with silence only for its unpaused part. [inferred]
- **Staging recovery.** Crash recovery and temp cleanup (`recoverCrashedRecordings`, `cleanupTempFiles`, `WavHeaderRepair.repairUnfinalized`) tell a crashed recording from a live one by how recently its track files were written (30 s). A paused recording writes nothing, so while paused the recorder refreshes the modification time of its track files (raw app temp and microphone WAV) every 10 s; a pause of any length is then never treated as a crash, and the raw app temp is never deleted under its writer. [inferred]
- **Crash during or after a pause:** crash recovery rescues the audio recorded before the crash as today; nothing from the pause exists on disk. The pause markers are not restored, because the pause log lives in memory until the stop. [inferred]
- **Recording ends while paused** (Stop, Stop Watching, meeting end, gh-54 countdown, monitored app quits, 4-hour cap): the recording is processed as usual and the open pause ends at the stop; its marker is the last line of the transcript. A recording paused from its first moment to its stop holds no audio and ends like any recording without audio today ("No audio data recorded", no job). [inferred]
- **4-hour cap** keeps counting wall-clock time, paused time included, so a forgotten paused recording still ends. [inferred]
- **gh-54 countdown during a pause:** the countdown runs unchanged; "Keep recording" does not resume a paused recording, and resuming does not answer the countdown. gh-54 estimates the cut position twice (from the recording's start and from its end); each estimate subtracts the paused time inside the interval it spans (start to cut, cut to stop). A cut time inside a pause lands at that pause's position, a pause that starts at or after the cut time is dropped from the job, and a pause still running at the cut time ends at the cut time. The record-only sidecar's stop time stays the cut time on the wall clock. [inferred]
- **Mac sleeping while recording:** the capture side stays exact (both tracks and the pause clock skip sleep alike, and the marker positions come from that clock). gh-54's cut works from wall-clock times and keeps its existing limitation that a sleep makes a cut keep more, never cut away speech. [inferred]
- **Timestamps.** Transcript line timestamps count recorded time and leave out pauses, while the marker shows wall-clock times; after a long pause the two no longer match, and the marker is what tells the reader why. Marker times are rendered in the time zone current at rendering time; a change of time zone or a daylight-saving switch between recording and a late re-diarization can shift them. [inferred]
- **Audio thread.** The pause timeline is read once per buffer on the capture threads and written only on pause and resume from the main thread, through the same short unfair-lock pattern the capture code already uses on those threads (`runningLock`, `actualSampleRateLock`); nothing is allocated while it is held. [inferred]
- **Live captions during a pause:** the caption sinks receive nothing, so no new caption appears; the overlay keeps the lines it showed before the pause (it is never saved). A caption still being formed at the pause can be finished with speech after the resume (display only, never saved); the live-caption flush is not used on pause because it retires the caption feeds for the rest of the recording. A live-caption drift warning can appear once in the log after a pause shorter than 5 s (log only). [inferred]
- **Echo detection, echo dedup, echo cancellation, diarization, VAD:** they run on the saved tracks, which hold the audio before and after each pause back to back and aligned; echo cancellation rewrites the microphone track in place at the same length, and VAD maps timestamps back to the file timeline, which is the timeline the markers are placed on. The legacy app-rate cross-check compares the two tracks' lengths, which lose the same span. [inferred]
- **Speaker-name re-apply** rewrites `] <label>:` in the saved transcript; a marker line has no such pattern and survives it. [inferred]
- **Several pauses** each get their own marker, in order. A very short pause still gets one (both times may read the same minute). [inferred]
- **Installer.** The fork's `mt-update` waits while a recording runs; a paused recording is still in `.recording`, so it waits for it too. [inferred]
- **Line caps.** `WatchLoop.swift`, `AppAudioCapture.swift`, `MicCaptureHandler.swift`, `DualSourceRecorder.swift` and `Tests/TestHelpers.swift` sit at or near the 600-line SwiftLint cap that `./scripts/lint.sh` enforces with `--strict`; new logic goes into extension files. [inferred]
- **App Store build:** the capture, loop, health, menu, icon and transcript parts compile into both variants; the API parts sit in files that are already `#if !APPSTORE` or used only by them. [inferred]

## Acceptance Criteria

- **R1:** While a recording's capture is running (auto-detected meeting, app recording or microphone-only recording), the menu bar menu shows "Pause Recording"; choosing it pauses the recording, the menu then shows "Resume Recording", the status line reads "Paused: <title>", and the menu bar icon shows a static pause symbol in place of the waveform. Choosing "Resume Recording" resumes the same recording and restores the recording icon. Each pause and resume writes one line to the on-disk diagnostic log (resume with the paused duration), without the meeting title. Errors: when no recording's capture is running (nothing recording, or a detected meeting whose recorder has not started yet) the item is absent. [paraphrase]
- **R2:** No audio captured between the pause instant and the resume instant, on either track, reaches the saved track files, the live captions, or anything made from them (mix, transcript, protocol, record-only files); the boundaries are exact to the frame as far as the capture's buffer timestamps go. Errors: if the recording ends while paused, nothing captured after the pause instant is saved. [paraphrase]
- **R3:** After a resume the recording continues as the same recording: the saved app and microphone tracks hold the audio before and after the pause back to back, with no silence inserted for the pause, and stay aligned to each other whatever their buffer sizes (the same span is removed from both, so an event captured on both tracks sits at the same saved position before and after any number of pauses, and the reported mic delay is unaffected by the pause). The recording yields one job, one transcript and one protocol. Errors: an unpaused recording is written exactly as today. [paraphrase]
- **R4:** Each pause appears in the transcript as its own line `[Pause HH:MM–HH:MM]` (local 24-hour start and end time, en dash) at the point on the transcript timeline where the recording was paused: before the first line that starts at or after it, never inside a merged same-speaker block. It is present in the transcription-stage transcript, the speaker-labelled transcript, a late re-diarization rebuild, the text the protocol is generated from, and the full transcript appended to the protocol. A pause still open when the recording ended shows the stop time as its end. Errors: a transcript with markers but no speech still fails as "Empty transcript", as today. [paraphrase]
- **R5:** A paused recording ends exactly like a running one: meeting detection ends a paused auto-detected recording (end grace and gh-54's countdown, which runs unchanged during a pause), a paused app recording ends when its app quits, Stop and Stop Watching work, and the 4-hour cap counts wall-clock time including paused time. When gh-54's countdown cuts the recording back, the cut lands at the cut time's position on the pause-free timeline, so no audio recorded after the cut time is kept even when pauses came before it, and the job's pauses are clipped at the cut. Errors: a failed cut is handled as gh-54 specifies (processed uncut, pauses unclipped). [paraphrase]
- **R6:** `POST /v1/record` accepts `pause` and `resume`, acting on whichever recording runs; `GET` and `POST /v1/record` report `paused` (true while the running recording is paused, whatever kind it is), the debug `/state` reports `recordingPaused`, and the `badge` value stays `recording` during a pause. `pause` while recording → 200 (already paused → 200 unchanged); `resume` while paused → 200 (not paused, or nothing recording → 200 unchanged). `docs/automation-api.md` and `docs/stream-deck.md` describe both verbs and the field. Errors: `pause` with no recording's capture running → 409 with the status body; an unknown verb → 400 as today. [paraphrase]
- **R7:** While a recording is paused, the channel-health checks are held: no red channel tint, no "Recording Appears Silent" warning, no "Capture Channel Silent", "Capture Channel Lost" or rebuilds-exhausted notification; after the resume they judge only what happens from the resume on, so none fires because of the pause, and a fault reported before the pause is not repeated. A pause of any length is never treated as a crashed recording by the staging recovery. Errors: a channel the capture gave up on during the pause is reported on the first check after the resume. [inferred]
- **R8:** In record-only mode the sidecar records each pause (`startedAt`, `endedAt`, `offsetSeconds`) under `pauses`, keeps `startedAt`/`stoppedAt` as wall-clock times, and carries schema version 3; processing that recording later on this Mac (file import) puts the pause markers into its transcript. Errors: a sidecar without `pauses` reads as a recording without pauses. [inferred]

If it fails, re-evaluate pausing by dropping buffers (against stopping and restarting the capture per pause) before continuing with the later tasks.
